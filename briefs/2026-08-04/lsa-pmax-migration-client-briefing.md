# LSA → Performance Max Migration: Client & Partner Briefing

*Drafted 2026-08-04. Source research below — verify against Google's official
notices for each account before sending, since rollout is account-by-account.*

## TL;DR

Google is folding Local Services Ads into Google Ads, running LSA campaigns
as a new "Performance Max for lead gen" campaign type. The standalone LSA
dashboard is going away. Rollout starts **this month (August 2026)** for a
specific set of home/storefront service categories, then expands through
2027. This is a real, live operational change for every LSA Command client —
not a future hypothetical — so it needs to go out in the next round of
client and partner communications, including the Monk Marketing white-label
line.

## What's changing

- **New home:** Local Services campaigns move into the main Google Ads
  interface as a specialized Performance Max campaign type built for
  pay-per-lead goals. The separate LSA dashboard is retired for migrated
  accounts.
- **First wave (August 2026):** Plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance
  repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control, and moving.
  Service-area businesses without storefronts and accounts on custom
  bidding/booking setups follow in a later wave.
- **Notice period:** Google is giving affected accounts a 14-day advance
  notice, then a 7-day reminder before the switch.
- **What stays the same:** Still pay-per-lead (not per-click), ads still
  only show on Search and Maps, campaigns stay keywordless and pull from
  the Business Profile.
- **What's new:** Real-time sync from Google Business Profile — business
  name, address, hours, and photos update automatically instead of being
  entered separately in the LSA dashboard.
- **What's at risk:** Historical LSA performance reports do not carry over
  automatically. If a client wants their pre-migration lead/cost history,
  it needs to be exported before their account transitions.

## Why this matters for LSA Command specifically

- Every client relationship is built on "we manage your Google visibility
  for you" — a client finding out their dashboard disappeared from a
  billing email instead of from us is the exact scenario that erodes trust
  in a managed-service relationship.
- The white-label pipeline (Monk Marketing / Chris Monkaitis) multiplies
  this: his clients will ask him the same questions he'd otherwise ask us,
  so he needs the explanation before his clients do.
- Historical reporting loss is the one piece with a hard deadline per
  account (the 14-day notice window) — this is the item most likely to
  cause an angry email if missed.

## Talking points (for partner/client calls)

1. "Google is consolidating Local Services Ads into the main Google Ads
   platform — it's a dashboard and back-end change, not a pricing or
   targeting change. You still only pay for real leads."
2. "This is rolling out in phases starting this month, beginning with a
   specific set of service categories. We're tracking which of your
   accounts are affected and when."
3. "The one thing with a real deadline is historical reporting — once an
   account gets its migration notice, we pull and save the performance
   history before the cutover so nothing is lost."
4. "Nothing you need to do differently day to day — this is us handling
   the transition on the back end."
