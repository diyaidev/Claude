## The "all B vitamins" reality

- **Widely available (at home or lab):** B12, folate (B9).
- **Available at lab, sometimes at home (Everlywell):** B6.
- **Lab draw only, rarely ordered:** B1 (thiamine), B2 (riboflavin), B3 (niacin), B5 (pantothenic acid), B7 (biotin). These need venous blood, specialized handling, and add cost with limited clinical payoff for most people.
- **Smarter than chasing all 8:** pair **B12 + folate** with **homocysteine** and/or **MMA** (methylmalonic acid) — these are *functional* markers that reveal whether your B12/folate are actually doing their job at the cellular level. Request A Test and other lab-draw panels include these.

So "the most comprehensive B coverage possible" realistically means **B12, folate, B6, + homocysteine/MMA** — achievable via a lab-draw panel, partially via Everlywell at home. No self-draw kit does the full B-complex.

## Practical tips for accurate results (whichever you pick)

1. **Hold biotin (B7) supplements 48–72 hours before testing.** Biotin — common in hair/skin/nails and B-complex supplements — skews many immunoassays and can throw off vitamin, thyroid, and other results. This matters a lot given you supplement B vitamins.
2. **Draw fasting, in the morning.** Iron especially swings with food and time of day; morning fasted is the standard for comparable results over time.
3. **Interpret ferritin alongside inflammation (hs-CRP).** Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant — infection/inflammation falsely raises it and can mask true iron deficiency. SiPhox and the lab panels include CRP; pairing them gives a truer read.
4. **Test at the same time of day / same conditions each quarter** so your trends are apples-to-apples.
5. **Don't take a vitamin D or B12 dose right before testing** — it can transiently spike serum levels.
